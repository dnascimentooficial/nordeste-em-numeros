Nordeste em Números · 1998–2026

Is the Northeaststill the same?It has changed. And there is still a long way to go.

Every now and then I hear that Brazil's Northeast is the same as ever. That nothing has changed. So I checked the official numbers, from 2000 to today.

  • 43.6%of the rest of the country's wealth per person is what the Northeast has (2023)it was 38.8% in 2002
  • 44.7%of the Northeast's population lives in poverty (2024)it was 58.8% in 2012 · in Brazil, today, 26.5%

Everything here comes from IBGE (the national statistics office), INEP (the national education research agency), Ipea (the federal applied economic research institute) and Atlas Brasil (the Atlas of Human Development in Brazil). I estimate nothing myself. The gray bands in the charts show who governed the country in each period, only to place the dates in time. No number here says that a government caused a result.

01 / Wealth

“The Northeast is going nowhere.” Is it?

In short: it has moved, but slowly. The gap has narrowed and is still wide.

How much the Northeast has for every 100 reais per person in the rest of the country. The dashed line at 100% would be parity.

GDP per capita is the wealth produced divided by the population. In 2023, the Northeast's figure was 43.6% of the one recorded in Brazil outside the Northeast. In 2002, it was 38.8%. Change over the period: +4.8 percentage points.

02 / Economy

“The economy here doesn't grow.”

In short: it grows, and it has grown a little faster than the national average. The Northeast's slice of the national pie has changed little.

GDP volume

Index, first year = 100, price effects removed.

The volume index measures how much the economy grew without the effect of inflation. From 2002 to 2023, the Northeast's GDP volume index went from 100 to 163.4; Brazil's went to 158.2. The Northeast's share of national GDP went from 13.1% to 13.8%.

03 / Education

“Education in the Northeast hasn't improved.”

In short: it has improved. The early-grades score more than doubled and came close to the Brazilian average. In the late grades and in high school, the gain was smaller.

IDEB, early primary grades

IDEB, Brazil's basic-education quality index, is the schools' score from 0 to 10. It combines what students learn with how many advance a grade. In the early primary grades (1st to 5th grade), the Northeast went from 2.9 in 2005 to 6.0 in 2025. Brazil scored 6.3.

04 / Work and income

“There are no jobs here and nobody earns anything.”

In short: this one has a grain of truth. Unemployment fell, but it is still higher than in the rest of the country. Income per person rose and is still far below the rest of the country's.

Unemployment rate

% of the labor force. The PNAD through 2014 and the PNAD Contínua since 2012 are different surveys: the lines are not spliced.

In 2025, the unemployment rate in the Northeast was 7.9%, against 5.6% in Brazil. The region's household income per person was 57.5% of that of Brazil outside the Northeast in 2025 (53.2% in 2012).

05 / Human development

“Human development? Still at the bottom.”

In short: it rose, with a stumble in 2020 and 2021, and the gap with Brazil narrowed. It has not closed yet.

For the Northeast as a region, the series starts in 2012.

HDI-M, the municipal human development index, combines income, education and life expectancy into a score from 0 to 1. The Northeast's went from 0.687 in 2012 to 0.762 in 2024. Brazil's reached 0.805.

06 / Poverty

“The poor are in the same place as ever.”

In short: they are not. The share of poor people fell, and the share of extremely poor people fell more. The decline was not a straight line: in 2021, at the height of the pandemic, poverty rose again. And the Northeast still has far more poor people, proportionally, than Brazil.

Poverty

% of the population below $8.30 per day (2021 purchasing power parity).

The poverty line here is $8.30 per person per day, the one used in the tables IBGE publishes for the UN indicators. In 2024, 44.7% of the Northeast's population lived below it, against 26.5% in Brazil. In 2012, it was 58.8% in the region. In extreme poverty ($3.00 per day), the region had 9.1%.

About the author

Who I am

Executive director of the Laboratório Urbano Vivo de Vitória (LUV), the Vitória Living Urban Lab. For more than 17 years, I have helped professionals, companies and governments turn complex problems into executable decisions, using strategy, innovation, artificial intelligence and public policy.

More about my work at dnascimento.com.br

Why I did this study

I did this study out of anger, and also out of pride. Anger at a discourse that keeps repeating that the Northeast is the same as ever, erases the region's history and ignores what states and municipalities did and failed to do. Pride in a region that has changed more than people say, even if a long road remains. So I checked the official data, from 2000 to today, and the history, from the colonial era to now. Nobody gets a free pass here: municipalities, states and the federal government share the bill. Every number has its source and calculation in plain view, for anyone who wants to rebut it or understand it.
