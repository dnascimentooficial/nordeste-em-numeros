About the data

Image: Júlio Diógenes (composite of the beaches, forests and sertão (semiarid backcountry) of the Northeast)

Why I built this dashboard

Because I got tired of hearing that the Northeast is the same as ever, said with total certainty and not a single number in hand. I gathered the official data from 2000 to today, compared the Northeast with Brazil and with the rest of the country, and left everything open for anyone who wants to check.

This is not cheerleading. Where things improved, I show it. Where the gap is still wide, I show that too.

Image on the home page: Júlio Diógenes (composite of the beaches, forests and sertão (semiarid backcountry) of the Northeast).

How to read it

  • The gray bands show who governed the country. They place the dates in time. They do not give credit or blame to anyone.
  • When a survey changes (PNAD to PNAD Contínua, for example), the chart line breaks. Different surveys are not spliced together.
  • A year with no data is left blank. I do not invent a point to connect the line.

What is still missing, and why

  • 2024 GDP: IBGE had not yet released the 2024 Regional Accounts (Contas Regionais) on November 3, 2026.
  • 2007 GDP per capita: there is no published value to check the calculation against.
  • Income and poverty before 2012: the old PNAD series has not been incorporated yet.
  • Enrollments in 2003 and 2004: they exist, but INEP only published them as PDFs. In 2026.
  • HDI-M (municipal human development index) for the Northeast in 2000 and 2010: not published for the region.
  • Poverty line: confirmed by Ipeadata metadata, not by an IBGE publication.

Formulas I use

Most numbers are published ready-made by the source. I only do the calculations below, all of them declared and checked.

GDP per capita

  • GDP per capita = GDP at current prices (in thousands of reais) ÷ resident population, multiplied by 1,000 to get reais.
  • Each year's population is the one IBGE uses in the GDP per capita it publishes: the annual estimate, the 2010 Census in 2010 and the 2022 Census in 2022 and 2023.
  • IBGE does not publish GDP per capita by region and state in a machine-readable format. I checked the calculation against the values it releases, and the result of that check is in the project's verification dictionary.

Brazil outside the Northeast

  • Counts (population, GDP, enrollments, unemployed people, employed people, informal workers, labor force, total household income): Brazil − Northeast.
  • GDP per capita: (Brazil's GDP − Northeast's GDP) ÷ (Brazil's population − Northeast's population), multiplied by 1,000. It is the ratio of the differences, not the average of the states.
  • Unemployment rate: (unemployed in Brazil − in the Northeast) ÷ (labor force in Brazil − in the Northeast), as a percentage.
  • Informality rate: (informal workers in Brazil − in the Northeast) ÷ (employed people in Brazil − in the Northeast), as a percentage.
  • Household income per person: (total household income in Brazil − in the Northeast) ÷ (PNAD Contínua population of Brazil − of the Northeast), multiplied by 1,000.
  • When no published count exists to redo the calculation (poverty, Gini, IDEB, HDI-M, school dropout, earnings from work, GDP volume, internet access), the comparison is with Brazil as a whole.
  • IBID: the reference is the national average published by INPI, which weights the states by GDP per capita. I do not calculate Brazil outside the Northeast.

Comparison between territories

  • Ratio = value for the Northeast (or the state) ÷ value for the reference, as a percentage. 100% means parity; 50%, half.
  • In the site's sentences, the change over the period is the value for the last year with data minus the value for the first year with data, in percentage points.

Values in reais

  • Household income per person and earnings from work: constant reais, using IBGE's PNAD Contínua deflator, at average prices of the last year in the series.
  • GDP and GDP per capita: current values for each year. Comparisons use the ratio between territories in the same year, which needs no deflator.
  • GDP volume: chained index published by IBGE (first year = 100), with no calculation of mine.

What I do not do

  • I do not interpolate years with no data, project future years or estimate missing values.
  • I do not splice different surveys together (PNAD and PNAD Contínua, Census and estimates).
  • I do not average rates across states: aggregates always come from the counts.

The files behind the numbers

The site reads only the published database, exactly as it appears in the files on the Data page, with the date and sha256 of each one. Go to Data.

What I measure on this site

I use Umami, an open-source audience measurement tool, to learn how the site is read. It sets no cookies, records no sessions and collects no personal data, and I do not store anyone's IP address.

What it counts: pages visited, time on each page, country and device type, the site a visitor came from, and clicks on site features (cards downloaded, spreadsheets downloaded, charts enlarged, sections read). I use these numbers only to decide what to improve here.

Browsers with “Do Not Track” turned on are not counted. To opt out, set the localStorage key umami.disabled to 1. It works per browser, on that device only.

Why I did this study

I did this study out of anger, and also out of pride. Anger at a discourse that keeps repeating that the Northeast is the same as ever, erases the region's history and ignores what states and municipalities did and failed to do. Pride in a region that has changed more than people say, even if a long road remains. So I checked the official data, from 2000 to today, and the history, from the colonial era to now. Nobody gets a free pass here: municipalities, states and the federal government share the bill. Every number has its source and calculation in plain view, for anyone who wants to rebut it or understand it.
