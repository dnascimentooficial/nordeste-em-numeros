How we got here

Northeast Brazil was once the economic center of the country. In eight eras, this page tells how the region fell behind and who decided what.

Image: Frans Post, 1660 (public domain)

What the censuses show

1872 46.7% of Brazilians lived in the Northeast

−19.8 points in 150 years

2022 26.9% in the 2022 Census

I chose population because it is the one measure we can calculate from the first census, in 1872, to the latest. The Northeast gained people in every census. What shrank was its weight in the country, and the eras below explain why.

    1530–1654

    The sugar colony

    The Crown made sugar the colony's business, and the coast of Pernambuco and Bahia was where it happened.

    I start with sugar because it is what made the Northeast the center of the colony. The Portuguese Crown divided the coast into captaincies and, in 1549, placed the seat of the colonial government in Salvador. Sugar mills (engenhos) spread across Pernambuco and Bahia in the second half of the 16th century. In 1570 the chronicler Gandavo counted 60 mills in Brazil, two-thirds of them in those two captaincies. A little more than ten years later, there were 120.

    The people who made that money were enslaved Africans, brought by force to plant, cut and grind cane. The profit stayed with a few. In the region, it went to the mill owners (senhores de engenho), who owned the land and the people. Outside the region, it went to those who carried the sugar to Europe, a trade in which the Dutch had weight. The model of settlement was decided in Lisbon. Concentrated land and slave labor were sustained by those who ruled here.

    In 1630 the Dutch West India Company took Olinda and Recife. The war lasted until 1654 and destroyed mills. By Roberto Simonsen's figures, sugar exports from the occupied captaincies fell by half after 1630. The Dutch were driven out by forces from the region itself. When they left, they took with them what they had learned here, and that weighed on the next era.

    Milestones

    1. 1549 Salvador becomes the seat of the colonial government. The Crown sets up the colony's government in Bahia.
    2. 1570 Gandavo counts 60 sugar mills. Two-thirds of them are in Pernambuco and Bahia.
    3. 1630 The Dutch take Olinda and Recife. The Dutch West India Company occupies Pernambuco until 1654.

    Numbers

    • from 60 to 120 Sugar mills in Brazil, counted in 1570 and between 1583 and 1585. historian's estimate Source: Stuart B. Schwartz, table 1, with the counts by Gandavo (1570) and Fernão Cardim (1583 to 1585).
    • from 60,000 to 30,000 crates Annual sugar exports from the captaincies occupied by the Dutch, before and after 1630. historian's estimate Source: Roberto Simonsen, História Econômica do Brasil (1500/1820) (Economic History of Brazil), 6th edition.

    What it left behind

    The Northeast was the center of the colony, but the wealth stayed with a few owners of land and people. That pattern of concentrated land lasted through the following centuries. interpretation

    1654–1808

    Competition, gold and the capital that moves

    Sugar gets a competitor in the Caribbean, gold from Minas pulls the colony toward the Center-South, and the capital leaves Salvador.

    After 1654, Northeast sugar had a competitor. The Dutch took technology and money to the Caribbean. Celso Furtado records that, less than ten years after the expulsion, a sugar economy of considerable size was already running there. The Northeast kept producing sugar, but now it shared the market with newer production.

    Historians disagree about how bad the damage was. Some speak of a sugar crisis, and others see a recovery lasting into the first decades of the 18th century. I will not pick a side. What nobody disputes is that the colony's center of gravity moved.

    Gold mining began in Minas Gerais at the end of the 17th century. Gold drew people, enslaved workers and the attention of the Crown, and it made Rio de Janeiro the port of the new wealth. In 1763 the capital moved from Salvador to Rio. Among other things, the move served to watch the mines closely and curb smuggling. It was a decision made in Lisbon, with gold in mind.

    In the region, the way of producing hardly changed. The mill owners kept land concentrated and kept slave labor, the same arrangement as before, now at a more contested price. The era ends in 1808, when the Portuguese court arrives in Rio and the political center moves to the Southeast for good.

    Milestones

    1. 1654 The Dutch leave Recife. The surrender, in January, ends the occupation. Caribbean sugar enters the competition soon after.
    2. 1661 Peace of The Hague. Portugal pays an indemnity to the Netherlands, which gives up on the Northeast.
    3. 1763 The capital moves from Salvador to Rio. Rio, the port for Minas gold, becomes the seat of the colonial government.

    Numbers

    • about 877 metric tons Gold produced in Brazil in the 18th century, in the most widely cited estimate. historian's estimate Source: Virgílio Noya Pinto, O ouro brasileiro e o comércio anglo-português (Brazilian Gold and Anglo-Portuguese Trade), 2nd edition, 1979.

    What it left behind

    The center of decision-making went to the Southeast in 1763 and never came back. From then on, much of what was decided about the Northeast was decided far from it. interpretation

    1808–1889

    Empire and revolts

    The court settles in Rio, Pernambuco rebels, coffee takes sugar's place and the Northeast sells people to the South.

    With the court in Rio, the northern provinces began paying taxes that supported the new capital and the war in the South. This came on top of the drought of 1816 and poor prices for sugar and cotton. In 1817 Pernambuco rebelled. The base of the revolt was an alliance of sugar and cotton producers with Recife merchants. In 1824 came the Confederation of the Equator. The central government put down both.

    Over the century, coffee from the Paraíba Valley, and later from São Paulo, took sugar's place in exports. When the Atlantic slave trade ended in 1850, the price of an enslaved person rose on the coffee plantations. Owners in the Northeast sold people there. Robert Slenes estimates that at least 200,000 people were taken to the Southeast through this internal trade, between the 1850s and the 1880s. The sale was the decision of those who owned people here. The demand came from there.

    The Great Drought of 1877 to 1879 killed tens of thousands of people in Ceará alone. Rodolpho Theóphilo counted 27,378 dead in Fortaleza in a little over two months. For the whole region, the estimates diverge so much that I prefer not to use any. Slavery ended only in 1888, one year before the end of the Empire, and the Northeast reached the Republic with less weight in the country's economy than it had at the start of the century.

    Milestones

    1. 1817 Pernambuco Revolution. Taxes for the court, the drought of 1816 and bad prices push Pernambuco to revolt.
    2. 1850 End of the Atlantic slave trade. The sale of enslaved people from the Northeast to the coffee plantations begins.
    3. 1877 Great Drought. The drought lasts until 1879 and kills tens of thousands of people in Ceará.

    Numbers

    • at least 200,000 Enslaved people taken to the Southeast through the internal slave trade, from the 1850s to the 1880s. historian's estimate Source: Robert Slenes, doctoral dissertation (Stanford, 1976), cited in the journal Resgate (Unicamp).
    • tens of thousands Deaths in the Great Drought of 1877 to 1879, in Ceará alone, as a floor. In Fortaleza alone, there were 27,378 in a little over two months, by Rodolpho Theóphilo's count. historian's estimate Source: María Verónica Secreto, A seca de 1877-1879 no Império do Brasil (The drought of 1877-1879 in the Empire of Brazil), História, Ciências, Saúde – Manguinhos, vol. 27, no. 1, 2020, p. 36, citing Rodolpho Theóphilo.

    What it left behind

    Under the Empire, the Northeast lost exports to coffee and lost people, sold to the South. The drought of 1877 fixed the region's image as a place of disaster, and that image still weighs on it. interpretation

    1889–1930

    First Republic: café com leite politics, drought and Canudos

    Federal power stays with São Paulo and Minas, industry is born in the Southeast and drought gets a federal agency.

    Under the Republic, the federal government stayed in the hands of São Paulo and Minas Gerais, and economic policy revolved around coffee. This arrangement is known as "café com leite" (coffee with milk) politics. Industry was born near the coffee. By Wilson Cano's figures, São Paulo's share of the country's industrial output rose from about one-sixth in 1907 to more than one-third in 1929. The Northeast went the other way. According to the same author, the region accounted for about 17% of the country's industrial output in 1907, and by 1939 that had fallen to about 10%.

    In the backlands of Bahia, the settlement of Canudos was destroyed by the Army in 1897, in the fourth expedition sent against it. The death tolls in circulation have no academic origin that I was able to verify, so I give no number here.

    Drought became a federal matter in 1909, with the Inspectorate of Works Against Drought, which would later become the DNOCS (National Department of Works Against Drought). The agency was run from Rio and built reservoirs and roads. Many reservoirs ended up inside large estates. In the backlands, the ones in charge were the coronéis, local bosses who controlled the vote and the land and now came to control the water too (the system known as coronelismo). Decades later, the report of the GTDN, a federal working group on Northeast development, would criticize this arrangement.

    From the outside, the era had a federal government turned toward coffee. From the inside, it had local rule that took hold of the water from public works.

    Milestones

    1. 1897 Canudos is destroyed. The Army's fourth expedition ends the war in October.
    2. 1909 The Inspectorate of Works Against Drought is born. Decree 7,619 creates the first federal agency against drought.
    3. 1919 The inspectorate becomes federal in name. Decree 13,687 creates the Federal Inspectorate of Works Against Drought.

    Numbers

    • from 15.9% to 37.5% São Paulo's share of Brazil's industrial output, from 1907 to 1929. historian's estimate Source: Wilson Cano, Desequilíbrios regionais e concentração industrial no Brasil 1930-1970 (Regional Imbalances and Industrial Concentration in Brazil, 1930-1970), p. 199.
    • from about 17% to about 10% The Northeast's share of Brazil's industrial output, from 1907 to 1939. historian's estimate Source: Wilson Cano, Desequilíbrios regionais e concentração industrial no Brasil 1930-1970, p. 56.

    What it left behind

    Drought became federal policy in 1909, but public works reached first the places that already had power. And the country's industry grew far from the Northeast. interpretation

    1930–1950

    Vargas and the great migration

    The federal government centralizes, industry grows in the Center-South and Northeasterners begin leaving for São Paulo and Rio.

    After 1930 the federal government began to decide more, and industry accelerated in the Center-South. For the Northeast, what was new was drought entering the Constitution. The 1934 Constitution ordered the federal government to spend at least 4% of its tax revenue not earmarked for other uses on defense against drought.

    The drought of 1932 showed the other side. Governments set up concentration camps to keep drought migrants (retirantes) away from the capitals, and the newspaper Correio da Manhã reported on the camps that year. Drought was treated as a public-order problem, and the drought migrant as a threat.

    It was in this period that Northeasterners began leaving for São Paulo and Rio, a flow that grew in the following decades. Drought pushed them out, and so did concentrated land. Farm work was precarious, and emergency work crews and water were under the control of local elites. I looked for a reliable number for how many left in those years and did not find one. So I give none.

    In 1945 the federal government created Chesf, the São Francisco hydroelectric company, to generate power at the Paulo Afonso falls, and the anti-drought inspectorate became the DNOCS. Power and water now had a federal company and a federal department. The land stayed with the same owners.

    Milestones

    1. 1932 Drought and concentration camps. Governments hold drought migrants in camps, far from the capitals.
    2. 1934 Drought enters the Constitution. Article 177 sets aside part of federal revenue for defense against drought.
    3. 1945 Chesf and DNOCS are born. In October, a decree-law creates Chesf. In December, the anti-drought inspectorate becomes the DNOCS.

    Numbers

    • 4% Minimum federal spending on defense against drought, as a share of its tax revenue not earmarked for other uses, set by the 1934 Constitution. official data Source: 1934 Constitution, art. 177.

    What it left behind

    This was when leaving became a destiny for generations of Northeasterners, and the Southeast grew also on that labor. interpretation

    1950–1964

    BNB, GTDN and Sudene

    For the first time, the Northeast's backwardness is treated as an economic problem, with a bank, a diagnosis and a development agency.

    The droughts of 1951 to 1952 and of 1958 showed the limits of reservoirs. In 1952 a law created the Banco do Nordeste (the Northeast's regional development bank), headquartered in Fortaleza. In 1955 Chesf inaugurated the first Paulo Afonso power plant.

    In 1959 came the report of the Working Group for the Development of the Northeast, the GTDN, coordinated by Celso Furtado. The diagnosis was blunt. Income per person in the Northeast was less than a third of that in the Center-South. The report pointed to the "relative scarcity of the land factor and lower capital accumulation" and concluded that the cause of the backwardness lay "in the relative poverty of its physical base." The proposal was to industrialize the region.

    The report also looked inward. The reservoir policy had served large estates. In the same year, Antônio Callado's reporting in the Correio da Manhã gave a name to the network of landowners and politicians who lived off anti-drought works: the "indústria da seca," the drought industry. The name stuck.

    In December 1959 a law established Sudene, the Northeast development agency. In the early 1960s came income tax rebates for those who invested in Northeast projects, a system that became known as 34/18, after the article numbers of the two laws.

    The federal government decided: the bank, the agency and the incentives. But the diagnosis that justified all of it said that part of the problem lay in the region itself.

    Milestones

    1. 1952 The Banco do Nordeste is born. Law 1,649, of July, creates the bank, headquartered in Fortaleza.
    2. 1955 Paulo Afonso starts generating power. Chesf inaugurates the first plant at the São Francisco River falls.
    3. 1959 GTDN and Sudene. The report coordinated by Furtado comes out, and in December Law 3,692 establishes Sudene.
    4. 1961 34/18 begins. Law 3,995 creates the income tax rebate for investing in the Northeast. Law 4,239, of 1963, expands it.

    Numbers

    • less than a third Income per person in the Northeast relative to the Center-South, in the GTDN's diagnosis. official data Source: GTDN, Uma política de desenvolvimento econômico para o Nordeste (An economic development policy for the Northeast), 1959.

    What it left behind

    For the first time, the Northeast's backwardness was treated as an economic problem with a known cause, not as a punishment from the climate. The BNB and Sudene exist today because of that turn. interpretation

    1964–1985

    Military dictatorship: big projects and a hollowed-out Sudene

    Furtado is stripped of his rights, Sudene loses its planning role and part of the tax incentives goes to the Amazon.

    In April 1964 Celso Furtado had his political rights suspended under Institutional Act No. 1 and left Sudene. The agency continued to exist, but lost its role of planning the region. Decisions began to come from Brasília.

    In 1970 a decree-law created the National Integration Program and sent 30% of the tax incentives to it. The program's first stage was to open the Transamazônica and Cuiabá-Santarém highways. In practice, part of the income tax rebate that used to go to Northeast projects began paying for roads in the Amazon. How much this actually took from the Northeast, I could not measure. The rule, however, is in the text of the decree-law. In 1974 the region's incentives were gathered into a fund, Finor.

    Factories and big projects came, and the per capita income gap with the rest of the country stayed large. Measuring that gap in the 1960s and 1970s is hard: estimates vary so much by source that a single number would mislead.

    The harshest critique of this model came from an insider. Francisco de Oliveira, who worked at Sudene from 1959 to 1964, published Elegia para uma re(li)gião (Elegy for a Re(li)gion) in 1977, a book against the path Sudene took, especially after the coup.

    Milestones

    1. 1964 Furtado loses his political rights. In April, under Institutional Act No. 1, he leaves Sudene.
    2. 1970 National Integration Program. Decree-Law 1,106 sends 30% of the tax incentives to the program that starts with the Transamazônica.
    3. 1974 Finor is born. Decree-Law 1,376 gathers the Northeast's incentives into one fund.

    Numbers

    • 30% Share of the tax incentives that the 1970 decree-law sent to the National Integration Program. official data Source: Decree-Law 1,106, of June 16, 1970, art. 5.

    What it left behind

    The region gained factories and big projects, but lost the place where its own development was thought out. The income gap with the rest of the country went through the military dictatorship without closing. interpretation

    1985–2026

    From redemocratization to today

    The Constitution creates regional funds, Sudene is abolished and returns, and the region produces a smaller share of GDP than its share of the population.

    The 1988 Constitution set aside 3% of what the federal government collects in income tax and IPI (the federal tax on manufactured goods) to finance the productive sector of the North, Northeast and Center-West. A 1989 law regulated the funds. The Northeast's fund, the FNE, is run by the Banco do Nordeste, and half of the money goes to the semiarid region. It is a fixed share of revenue, written into the Constitution.

    In 2001 Sudene was abolished and replaced by an agency, Adene. In 2007 a complementary law recreated Sudene. The institution born from the 1959 diagnosis disappeared and came back within six years.

    In 2022, according to IBGE's Regional Accounts, the Northeast produced 13.8% of the country's GDP. IBGE warns that this series will still be revised, with a different reference year. The share of the population that lives here, at the top of this page, is almost double that. Put another way: the Northeast's GDP per capita is close to half of Brazil's.

    The money from the funds and the incentives comes from a federal decision. But it does not apply itself. It passes through state governments, banks and companies in the region itself, which decide where it ends up. I do not put the results of this era on any government's account.

    Milestones

    1. 1988 Constitution creates the regional funds. Article 159 sets aside 3% of income tax and IPI to finance the North, Northeast and Center-West.
    2. 1989 Law 7,827 regulates the FNE. Half of the Northeast fund goes to the semiarid region.
    3. 2001 Sudene abolished. A provisional measure replaces the agency with Adene.
    4. 2007 Sudene recreated. Complementary Law 125, of January 3, recreates the agency.

    Numbers

    • 3% Share of federal income tax and IPI revenue that the 1988 Constitution set aside to finance the productive sector of the North, Northeast and Center-West. official data Source: 1988 Constitution, art. 159, item I, subitem c.
    • 13.8% The Northeast's share of Brazil's GDP in 2022. official data Source: IBGE, Regional Accounts 2022 (SIDRA, table 5938).

    What it left behind

    The gap that began to form when the economic center left the Northeast is still in today's numbers. More than one in four Brazilians lives here, and the region produces less than one-seventh of what the country produces. interpretation

About the author

Who I am

Executive director of the Laboratório Urbano Vivo de Vitória (LUV), the Vitória Living Urban Lab. For more than 17 years, I have helped professionals, companies and governments turn complex problems into executable decisions, using strategy, innovation, artificial intelligence and public policy.

More about my work at dnascimento.com.br

Why I did this study

I did this study out of anger, and also out of pride. Anger at a discourse that keeps repeating that the Northeast is the same as ever, erases the region's history and ignores what states and municipalities did and failed to do. Pride in a region that has changed more than people say, even if a long road remains. So I checked the official data, from 2000 to today, and the history, from the colonial era to now. Nobody gets a free pass here: municipalities, states and the federal government share the bill. Every number has its source and calculation in plain view, for anyone who wants to rebut it or understand it.
