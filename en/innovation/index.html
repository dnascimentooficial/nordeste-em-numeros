02 / Knowledge

PhDs, graduate programs and state spending on science

In short: the base has grown a lot, but it is still not the size of the region. The Northeast produces 16.7% of the country's PhDs and has 27% of Brazilians.

PhDs awarded

Northeast as % of Brazil. Through 2016, only programs with a grade of 3 or higher; the two series do not link up.

Northeast share of PhDs awarded in Brazil. The ruler next to the text compares each share with the population's.

In 1998, Northeast programs awarded 83 PhDs. In 2016, 2,956. Since 2017, when CAPES (the federal graduate-education agency) stopped filtering programs by grade, the count is different: 3,372 in 2017 and 4,328 in 2024, 16.7% of Brazil. Graduate programs in the region with a grade of 3 or higher went from 179 in 1998 to 846 in 2016; counting all of them, there were 962 in 2024. And the Northeast's share of what state governments spend on science and technology went from 4.9% in 2000 to 11.9% in 2019, when the MCTI series (the federal science ministry's data) ends.

03 / Innovation

Patents, innovating industry and the IBID

In short: innovation output has grown and is still small for the size of the region: 14.2% of resident patents, for 27% of the population. The industry that exists innovates at the country's pace.

Patent applications by residents

Northeast as % of Brazil, by the state of the first applicant. 2024 and 2025 are partial: they may still grow.

Northeast share of patent applications by residents of Brazil (inventions and utility models).

In 2000, Northeast residents filed 325 invention and utility-model patents, 5.2% of applications by Brazilian residents. In 2025, there were 1,352, 14.2%. In industry, the latest PINTEC (IBGE's industrial innovation survey), from 2017, found 4,278 companies that innovated out of the Northeast's 12,654, or 33.8%. In Brazil, 33.9%. Industry here innovates at the same rate; what is missing is industry.

04 / Work

Jobs in information technology

In short: IT jobs have grown, but the Northeast has 9.8% of them, little more than a third of its population's weight.

Salaried jobs in IT

Northeast as % of Brazil, CNAE 62 and 63. The new CEMPRE table, from 2022, does not link up with the old one.

Northeast share of salaried staff at IT and information services companies (CNAE 62 and 63).

05 / Digital government and parks

Digital government and technology parks

Outside the official statistics: an industry-association index and an MCTI survey

Digital government: Piauí left the bottom of the list

  • 26th → 1stPiauí in the ABEP-TIC digital public services index (2021 → 2025)
  • 2 of 8states with a Gold Seal that are in the Northeast: Piauí and Pernambuco (2026)
  • 67.8 and 69.3points, Northeast and Brazil average, on a scale of 0 to 100 (2026)

The index measures what each state government offers in public services online. Piauí was among the last from 2020 to 2023 (22nd, 26th, 25th and 22nd), rose to 3rd in 2024, shared 1st place with Rio Grande do Sul in 2025 and got the top score, 100, in 2026.

The rest of the region did not move along with it. In 2026, ABEP-TIC (the association of state IT agencies) replaced the ranking with seals: Piauí and Pernambuco got Gold, Ceará got Silver, Bahia got Bronze, and Alagoas, Paraíba, Maranhão, Sergipe and Rio Grande do Norte got no seal. Rio Grande do Norte and Alagoas received the Gold Progress seal, given to those that advanced the most from one year to the next.

Bahia took the opposite path from Piauí: it was 2nd in 2021, stayed in the top five through 2024 and fell to 16th in 2025.

Technology parks: the Northeast has 18% of the companies installed

  • 113technology park initiatives in Brazil; only 64 are operating (Jan 2025)
  • 2,706companies and organizations installed in operating parks (Jan 2025)
  • 18%of them are in the Northeast; in the South, 45%, and in the Southeast, 33% (Jan 2025)

The MCTI survey, launched at the Anprotec (the national association of innovation environments) conference on October 13, 2025, counts every initiative that presents itself as a technology park, including those still on paper: of the 113, another 42 are being implemented and 7 are in planning.

The Northeast, with more than a quarter of the country's population, hosts 18% of the companies and organizations installed in these parks. Pernambuco has 9% of the national total and Rio Grande do Norte, 5%. In January 2025, Maranhão and Piauí had no park in operation.

To narrow this gap, a 2024 MCTI call with Finep (the federal innovation funding agency) and the FNDCT (the national science and technology fund) put R$ 100 million into parks in states that had few or none, among them Alagoas, Maranhão, Piauí and Sergipe. Adding up the calls, Finep and the FNDCT committed about R$ 670 million to 56 parks.

The book shows how many parks each state has only in a chart, without the numbers written out, so that count is not included here. And the data is reported by the parks themselves: in 2023, 43 of the 64 in operation answered the survey.

Source: MCTI, book Evolução, Impacto e Potencial dos Parques Tecnológicos do Brasil (Evolution, Impact and Potential of Brazil's Technology Parks) (2025), preface and sections 2.1 and 2.2, with the MCTI-InovaData-Br database of January 2025; survey by the UFV Management Technologies Center. Data reported by the parks themselves.

06 / Porto Digital

Porto Digital up close: 25 years of an innovation district in Recife

Some of the numbers are Porto Digital's own; some are public data (Novo CAGED and INEP) cut by the Porto Digital Observatory

Porto Digital was born in 2000 as a public policy to foster the digital economy in Pernambuco, bring together government, universities and companies, keep talent in the state and revitalize the Bairro do Recife district. There are other innovation environments in the region. This one gets the closer look because, through its own observatory, it publishes numbers that can be checked against public data. Measuring all the others would require the MCTI's list by state, which is not yet accessible.

The size, according to Porto Digital itself

  • R$ 7.4 bnin annual revenue of the ecosystem's companies (2025)
  • 541companies on board
  • 24,000employees (2025)
  • 230,000 m²of historic buildings restored since 2000, across 171 hectares of downtown Recife

These numbers are self-reported by Porto Digital, which does not publish its method. The website lists 541 companies; the Observatory's e-book, from September 2026, says more than 540 in 2025. The district also operates in Caruaru, in the Agreste, with the Armazém da Criatividade, winner of the 2025 Finep Innovation Award in the Innovation Environment category.

Source: Porto Digital, pages Sobre o Porto Digital (About Porto Digital) and Prêmios e reconhecimentos (Awards and recognition) (portodigital.org); e-book Ecossistema em Estudo (Ecosystem Under Study), by the Porto Digital Observatory (September 2026), p. 2.

Formal employment

  • 14,200formal technology jobs in Recife (June 2026)
  • 72%of them in software development; in the Northeast, 30.9%
  • +13.3%in 12 months; Recife's economy grew 3.8% and Brazil's ICT average, 2.2% (through June 2026)

The public database counts only people with a formal work contract, and it reaches 14,200 technology jobs in all of Recife. That is far fewer than the 24,000 employees Porto Digital reports, and Porto Digital does not say what goes into its count.

What stands out is the profile. In Recife, 72% of these jobs are in software development and licensing: 10,254 of them, the largest number among Northeast capitals. In the region, development accounts for 30.9% of ICT employment, and the rest is support and consulting.

And the pace. In the 12 months through June 2026, formal ICT employment in Recife grew 13.3%. Over the same period, Florianópolis had 1.8%, Curitiba 1.3% and Belo Horizonte 1.0%.

Source: Novo CAGED (Ministry of Labor and Employment), as read by the Porto Digital Observatory, e-book Ecossistema em Estudo (September 2026), p. 4 to 9. It is not the same count as the IT jobs in chapter 4, which come from IBGE's Central Business Register.

Training has doubled

  • 703 → 1,427graduates per year from higher-education ICT courses in Recife (2020–2022 average → 2024)
  • +103%in Recife; in Brazil, the increase was 26%
  • 64%of the city's new professionals come from colleges partnered with Porto Digital

Porto Digital attributes the jump to two programs. Residência Tecnológica (Technology Residency) is a required course at partner colleges in which students work on real company problems. Embarque Digital (Digital Boarding), created with the Recife city government, gives full scholarships in technology courses to people who attended public school, with half the slots reserved for Black and mixed-race (pretos e pardos) students.

According to the program itself, dropout in Embarque Digital is below 40%, against 60% on the national average, and 70% of students finish the course employed.

Source: Censo da Educação Superior (Higher Education Census) (INEP), as read by the Porto Digital Observatory, e-book Ecossistema em Estudo, p. 18 and 19. Dropout and employability: Embarque Digital data.

Who is being hired

  • 57%of ICT hires in Recife were Black, mixed-race, Indigenous and Asian-descendant people (H1 2026)
  • 28%gender pay gap in hiring salaries; in Florianópolis, 73% (H1 2026)
  • 26.8%of IT hires in Pernambuco were under 25; a year earlier, 24.7% (H1 2026)

In the hubs of the South, the share of Black, mixed-race, Indigenous and Asian-descendant people in new technology jobs does not reach 33%, according to the same study.

The pay gap is the simple average of the hiring salary in the half-year, without separating job title or experience. It serves to compare cities, not to measure discrimination.

The data on young people runs against a thesis that has circulated since ChatGPT: that artificial intelligence would end the entry-level job. In Pernambuco, the share of hires under 25 increased.

Source: Novo CAGED (MTE), hires in the first half of 2026 and of 2025, as read by the Porto Digital Observatory, e-book Ecossistema em Estudo (September 2026), p. 11 to 15.

In short: Porto Digital shows what public policy delivers when it lasts 25 years. But one neighborhood in Recife is not the Northeast: the region still has less than a tenth of the country's IT jobs, as chapter 4 shows.
